About
Hi, I'm
Zainab!

I'm a third-year Computer Science student at the University of Washington, minoring in Data Science. I got into this work by noticing the small frustrations in the apps I already used, and wondering what a kinder version might look like. That curiosity turned into brew., WIIRED Studio, and a habit of designing things until they're worth building. I care about people-centered problems: cafés that are easier to discover, hackathons that are easier to run, and data that's easier to understand.

How I Work
How I work.
  • Start with what breaksI start every project with its messy parts: the confusing form, the lost place, the dead end, then design so people can recover without ever feeling like they broke something.
  • Design for recovery in brew.In brew., that meant building real save states and clear feedback into a café discovery app, not just a nice-looking prototype.
  • Build to learnI move quickly between Figma and code because building is where I actually learn: a flow that reads perfectly on screen, like Habitat's, often needs its timing and edge cases reworked once real people touch it.
  • Think in systemsI came to design through computer science, so I think about the system underneath the interface, not just its surface. My best work happens shoulder to shoulder with engineering and real users.
Recovery-First Figma + Code Systems Thinking
How I Use AI
How I use AI.
  • Start with the ideaI use AI once I know what I am trying to make. The concept, product direction, and final decisions stay mine. AI helps me explore more possibilities and reach a working version faster.
  • Rapid prototypeI use Figma Make and Claude Design to turn rough ideas into testable interfaces quickly. This lets me pressure-test flows, interactions, and edge cases before polishing the wrong direction.
  • Build with AIClaude Code helps me move from mockup to real product, especially when I am scaffolding, debugging, or testing interactions that need actual state. I still review the logic, make the tradeoffs, and shape how the experience feels.
  • Keep human judgment (!!)I treat AI as a collaborator, not an autopilot. I question its output, rewrite anything generic, and keep only what supports the problem and the people I am designing for. Speed creates quickly, but care makes it worth keeping, and people can tell.
